# SCREENING — Fortune Cookie Funnies for Engineers

Brief criteria, in order (most important first):
1. **On-topic** — a fortune cookie message for a software engineer, not a general slogan or a joke about something else.
2. **Genuinely funny** — lands on a first read, real punchline, not a half-working pun.
3. **Fortune voice** — short, declarative, twist from the engineer's world (code, deploys, bugs, standups).
4. **Fresh** — not seen on a mug, sticker, or conference slide.
5. **Tight** — every word earns its place, funniest word last.

Scores 1–5 per criterion, in order. Char counts include spaces and punctuation.

## Rejected reflexes (stock — never submitted)

- "It works on my machine" as the whole joke — mug slogan, fails criterion 4 outright.
- "It's not a bug, it's a feature" — conference-slide fossil, fails 4.
- "There are only 10 kinds of people..." — binary-joke antique, fails 4 and 2.
- "Why do programmers prefer dark mode? Because light attracts bugs" — pun that only half works, fails 2.

## Winner: #1 — 25/25

> Your future holds a merge conflict with a teammate who left in 2019.

Why it wins: the seam is prophecy vs. office absurdity — a fortune cookie predicts your future, and the future is a merge conflict with someone who no longer works there. The punch word is the year, sitting last, and it does all the work: it makes the teammate's absence concrete and the conflict impossible to resolve by talking to them. One idea, one sentence, declarative, 68 of 120 characters. No stock phrase anywhere in it; no engineer has this on a mug. It lands on a first read because every engineer has opened a repo and found a ghost's name in git blame.

Runner-up #2 is nearly as funny but reads as an observation about the past, not a fortune about the reader's future, so it scores lower on criterion 3. Runner-up #3 is a fine escalation joke but "3 a.m. bug fix" is a familiar scene, so it loses on freshness.
